About Reactor Dynamics

A nuclear power plant you can run in the browser — built so you can learn what operators do and why the plant responds the way it does.

Nothing to install, no account. Desktop or laptop for now.

▶  Enter the Control Room

What this is

A pressurized-water plant you operate in the browser — from cold shutdown to full power on one continuous model. You can start with no nuclear background; the plant still behaves like a plant when you change load, trip the unit, or break something on purpose.

The point is to learn two things together:

  • What — plant operation: the board, the procedures, the order of a startup, how you hold power and recover from upsets.
  • Why — plant dynamics and the physics behind them: how heat, reactivity, inventory, steam, and the turbine couple, so the board makes sense instead of feeling like a black box.

You learn the what by running the plant. You learn the why from how it responds — and, if you want the model spelled out, from the Physics page and the in-sim manual.

It is not a score chase. You run the board.

What you can do

  • Bring a cold plant to life. Pressurize the system, start the reactor coolant pumps, heat to hot standby, pull rods to criticality, raise power, put the generator on the grid, and carry the plant to 100%.
  • Keep it online. Load changes, boration and dilution, feedwater and level control, holding steady through the slow chemistry of xenon.
  • Work plant casualties. Reactor trips and recovery, stuck valves, pump trips, steam generator tube leaks, loss of heat sink — injected on your terms, or arriving without warning.

The Three Mile Island sequence is here if you want it: stuck-open relief, inventory loss, and the recovery path that mattered. You can step into that event or work past it.

Every session uses the same plant model. An easier session is a gentler scenario, not a simpler plant.

Commercial-format operator manuals are in the control room. Free play is always available. A guided training campaign — missions that watch the board and walk real procedures — is available where the release offers it; otherwise free play and the manuals still stand alone.

The plant

A compact pressurized water reactor: about 100 MWe, one coolant loop, one steam generator — small enough to learn whole, complete enough to behave like a plant. Cold shutdown to full power is one continuous path; nothing is a cutscene.

Two more reactor types sit in the project — a boiling-water plant and a graphite-channel RBMK — with working physics engines. Their public control rooms are not ready yet.

Where it runs

Any modern desktop browser. The simulation runs on your machine — from this site, from a local copy, or as a single downloadable file that works with no internet connection.

Contact

Questions, bugs, or “this does not match plant behavior”? Email reactordynamics@gmail.com. In the simulator, Contact gives you that address and can download session diagnostics to attach.

Physics (the why)

Operation teaches the what. For the why in writing — what the model computes, how the systems couple, what numbers are pinned to published plant data, and what is left out — see Physics behind the simulator.
