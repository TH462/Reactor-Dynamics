About Reactor Dynamics

An educational nuclear power plant simulator that runs entirely in your browser.

Reactor Dynamics models three reactor types — a PWR (pressurized water reactor), a BWR (boiling water reactor), and an RBMK (the Chernobyl-era Soviet design) — accurately enough to teach real plant behavior, and to reproduce the conditions behind the three most famous nuclear accidents: Three Mile Island, Chernobyl, and Fukushima.

The defining idea: instruments, not truth

In most simulation games you see the true state of the machine. Here you don’t. You operate on instrument readings — which lag, drift, fail, and occasionally lie — never on the underlying physical state. That gap between what is true and what is indicated is what made the real accidents possible: at Three Mile Island, operators watched a valve indicator that read “closed” while the valve was stuck open, and drew reasonable conclusions from unreasonable data. Diagnosing a casualty through imperfect instruments is the skill this simulator is built to teach.

How faithful is it?

The physics engines model point kinetics with delayed neutron groups, reactivity feedback (fuel and moderator temperature, xenon, boron), decay heat, two-phase steam generator behavior, and a complete balance of plant — turbine, condenser, feedwater, and electrical output. Setpoints, alarms, and procedures follow commercial practice, and the included operator manuals are written in commercial format.

That said: this is an educational simulation, not engineering software. Models are simplified and tuned for teaching. It must not be used for real-world reactor operations, safety analysis, or as a substitute for licensed operator training.

Learning to operate

An instructor layer watches the board and walks you through guided campaigns: approach to criticality, power ascension, load maneuvers, plant cooldown, and casualty response. Missions are gated — the instructor checks the board state, not just your clicks. The campaign culminates in a story module that puts you in the control room for the night shift of March 28, 1979. The full operator manuals are built into the simulator’s Training tab.

How it’s built

Vanilla JavaScript, no framework, no build step, no server — the whole plant runs locally in your browser tab, so it works in restricted institutional environments. The PWR control room is playable today; the BWR and RBMK physics engines are complete, and their control rooms are under construction.

Feedback

Found a bug, or a place where the physics or procedures are wrong? Email reactordynamics@gmail.com. Inside the simulator, the 💬 button can download a session diagnostics file you can attach to the message.
