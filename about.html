About Reactor Dynamics

An educational nuclear power plant simulator with realistic, high-fidelity physics. Simple enough to teach someone new to nuclear power how a plant is operated — and serious enough that you can practice real maneuvers, failures, and recovery on the same model.

Nothing to install. It runs in your browser.

Why this exists

Most people never get near a control room. The few training tools that are “real” are built for licensed operators and are too heavy, too specialized, or simply unavailable. Games and demos that are available usually cut the physics until the plant cannot teach anything that survives contact with a real procedure.

This project sits in the middle on purpose:

  • Educational first. You should be able to sit down without a nuclear background and learn what the board is for: pressure, temperature, level, rods, boron, steam, feed, turbine, alarms, and the order of a startup.
  • Physics that stay honest. The model is not a cartoon power bar. Heat, reactivity, inventory, steam, and the balance of plant couple the way they do in a real PWR, so a load change, a heatup, a trip, or a leak has consequences you can reason about.
  • One plant, two depths. The same board that teaches “what is Mode 3?” is the board you use for a careful power ascension, a stuck relief valve, loss of feed, or a night of bad indications. You do not graduate out of the model — you go deeper into it.

If you want a score chase, this is the wrong product. If you want to understand and operate a plant, it is built for that.

Who it is for

  • People who have never operated anything nuclear and want the basics done right
  • Students and instructors who need a living plant, not a slide deck
  • Engineers and enthusiasts who want realistic maneuvers and failure practice without a full-scope training center
  • Anyone who wants to see why procedures are written the way they are, by running them

What you can do

Learn the basics of plant operation. Bring a cold plant up, pressurize, start pumps, heat to hot standby, approach criticality, raise power, connect the generator, and hold steady state. Commercial-format manuals sit in the room. A guided campaign can walk you through the path; free play lets you stay on the board as long as you need.

Practice real work. Load changes, boration and dilution, feed and level control, trips and recovery, and injected failures — stuck valves, pump trips, sensor problems, loss of heat sink. The plant does not reset its physics for beginners: simpler lessons use the same model as harder ones.

Playable plant today. A compact Westinghouse-style pressurized water reactor (on the order of 100 MWe), single loop, full path from cold shutdown to at-power operation. BWR and RBMK physics exist in the project; their control rooms are not ready for the public board yet.

The physics (what “high fidelity” means here)

The plant is a coupled, real-time model of the systems an operator actually runs. It is educational software — not a licensing code or a digital twin of a named unit — but the mechanisms that dominate operator decisions are modeled in enough depth that behavior stays prototypical.

Core and reactivity

  • Point kinetics with delayed neutrons — power does not jump; period and startup rate are real, and that is how you approach criticality safely.
  • Reactivity feedbacks that fight (or help) you — fuel temperature (Doppler), moderator temperature, soluble boron, and xenon. Pull rods and the plant answers; heat the fuel and reactivity falls; poison builds after high power and after a trip.
  • Rods and boron as the two main operator handles for criticality and long-term control.

Heat and the primary system

  • Fission power and decay heat both put energy into the coolant — the core is still a heat source after a scram.
  • Reactor coolant carries that heat to the steam generator; pump status and flow matter.
  • Pressurizer control of pressure and level (heaters, spray, relief) — inventory, temperature, and pressure stay linked the way they do on a real board.

Steam generator and secondary

  • Primary heat boils secondary water; steam flow, feedwater, and level are a continuous balance.
  • Main feed and auxiliary feed, dumps, and isolation change how heat leaves the plant.

Balance of plant

  • Turbine, condenser, generator, and grid — load is a steam demand. Change load and the primary feels it. Trip the turbine or lose the heat sink and the plant has to handle the energy another way.

Protection and automation

  • Trips, interlocks, and engineered safeguards act from what the instruments show, with lag, noise, and possible failure — the same rule real boards live under.
  • Charging, letdown, and boron systems move inventory and reactivity on operator and automatic timescales.

What you should expect. A plant that heats, cools, goes critical, makes steam, makes power, trips, and fails in ways you can explain with textbook PWR physics. You should not expect multi-dimensional core maps, full containment engineering suites, or a certified replica of one licensed unit. Fidelity here means operator-relevant realism, not infinite detail.

Instruments and truth

You run on instrument readings, not a god-view of every true variable. That is how plants are operated, and it is how some of the hardest lessons (including failures and historical accidents) actually work. The physics are still running underneath; the skill is operating through the board.

How it runs

Plain web technology: open it, run the plant locally in the browser on a desktop or laptop. No account required for the simulation itself. Campaigns, manuals, free play, and failure injection are in the control room.

Contact

Questions, bugs, or “this doesn’t match plant behavior”? Email reactordynamics@gmail.com. In the simulator, the 💬 button gives you that address and can download session diagnostics to attach.
