About Reactor Dynamics

A nuclear power plant you can run from your browser. Take a cold, quiet plant all the way to full power, hold it there through whatever goes wrong, and learn — hands on the controls — why real operators do things the way they do.

Nothing to install, no account. Desktop or laptop for now.

▶  Enter the Control Room

What this is

Real control-room training is locked inside licensed facilities, on machines the public never touches. Reactor Dynamics brings that kind of hands-on plant operation to anyone with a browser: plain enough to start with no nuclear background, and faithful enough that a load change or a stuck valve has consequences you can trace and reason about.

It is not a game about scores. It is a plant you operate.

What you can do

  • Bring a cold plant to life. Pressurize the system, start the reactor coolant pumps, heat to hot standby, pull rods to criticality, raise power, put the generator on the grid, and carry the plant to 100%.
  • Run it like a job. Load changes, boration and dilution, feedwater and level control, holding a steady board through the slow chemistry of xenon.
  • Work real casualties. Reactor trips and recovery, stuck valves, pump trips, failed sensors, steam generator tube leaks, loss of heat sink — injected on your terms, or arriving without warning.

That includes the classics. The Three Mile Island sequence is here — the stuck-open relief valve, the indicator that claimed it was shut, and the slowly draining reactor behind them both. You can walk into the same trap, or catch it in time.

Every session runs on the same physics, from first tutorial to worst casualty. There is no easy mode: an easier session is a gentler scenario, not a simpler plant.

Commercial-format operator manuals are in the control room, and a guided training campaign — an instructor that watches the board and gates your progress through real procedures — is rolling out alongside free play.

The plant

A compact pressurized water reactor: about 100 MWe, one coolant loop, one steam generator — small enough to learn whole, complete enough to behave like a plant. Cold shutdown to full power is one continuous path on one continuous model; nothing is a cutscene.

Two more reactor types are built into the project — a boiling-water plant and a Chernobyl-type RBMK — with working physics engines. Their control rooms open later.

Where it runs

Any modern desktop browser. The simulation runs entirely on your machine — from this site, from a local copy, or as a single downloadable file that works with no internet connection at all.

Contact

Questions, bugs, or “this does not match plant behavior”? Email reactordynamics@gmail.com. In the simulator, the Contact control gives you that address and can download session diagnostics to attach.
