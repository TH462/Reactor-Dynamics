Physics behind the simulator

A short account of what the plant model actually does. This is the technical companion to the About page.

The simulator is a real-time model of the systems an operator works with. It is built for training and teaching, not for licensing a plant or replacing a full engineering code. The goal is that heatup, load changes, trips, and failures behave in ways you can explain with ordinary reactor-plant physics — without claiming a one-to-one digital twin of any named unit.

Core and reactivity

  • Point kinetics with delayed neutrons — power does not jump instantly. Startup rate and reactor period are real controls; that is how you approach criticality without racing the core.
  • Feedbacks from the plant — fuel temperature (Doppler), coolant temperature, dissolved boron, and xenon. Pull rods and the plant answers; heat the fuel and reactivity falls; poison builds after high power and after a trip.
  • Rods and boron — the two main operator handles for criticality and slower long-term control.

Heat and the primary system

  • Fission power and decay heat both put energy into the coolant. After a scram the core is still a heat source.
  • Reactor coolant carries that heat to the steam generator. Pump status and flow matter.
  • The pressurizer holds pressure and level with heaters, spray, and relief. Inventory, temperature, and pressure stay linked the way they do on a real board.

Steam generator and secondary

  • Primary heat boils secondary water. Steam flow, feedwater, and level are one continuous balance.
  • Main feed, auxiliary feed, dumps, and isolation change how heat leaves the plant.

Turbine and grid

  • Turbine, condenser, generator, and grid connection close the loop. Electrical load is a steam demand: change load and the primary feels it. Trip the turbine or lose the heat sink and the energy has to go somewhere else.

Protection and automation

  • Trips, interlocks, and safeguards act from what the instruments show — with lag, noise, and possible failure — the same rule real boards live under.
  • Charging, letdown, and boron systems move inventory and reactivity on both operator and automatic timescales.

What you should expect

A plant that heats, cools, goes critical, makes steam, makes power, trips, and fails in ways that match textbook pressurized-water-reactor behavior for an operator.

You should not expect a full 3-D core map, a complete containment engineering suite, or a certified copy of one licensed unit. Detail is spent where the operator feels it; the rest is simplified on purpose.
