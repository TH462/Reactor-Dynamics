Physics behind the simulator

What the model actually computes, what its numbers are anchored to, and what is deliberately left out. Written for readers with a science or engineering background. What the simulator is for is on the About page.

In brief: six-group point kinetics · lumped primary and secondary · protection and automation on instrument channels · parameters fitted to published plant data · no spatial core, not a named digital twin.

Reactor Dynamics is a real-time, lumped-parameter model of a single-loop pressurized water reactor of roughly 100 MWe. Core, primary loop, pressurizer, steam generator, turbine-generator, and the control and protection systems advance as one coupled system in sub-second steps — real time or accelerated. It is built for training, not licensing. The bar we aim for: operator-visible behavior should match ordinary plant physics, and pinned numbers should come from published plant data rather than guesswork.

Core and reactivity

  • Point kinetics, six delayed-neutron groups, with standard U-235 group constants (βeff ≈ 0.65%). Reactor period and startup rate fall out of the kinetics and drive the source- and intermediate-range nuclear instrumentation, so approaching criticality is an exercise in rate control, not a scripted event.
  • Reactivity feedbacks: Doppler (fuel temperature), moderator feedback that tracks coolant density rather than a constant temperature coefficient, dissolved boron, and xenon.
  • Xenon as an explicit decay chain — I-135 → Xe-135 with their real half-lives (6.6 h and 9.1 h): production, decay, and flux burnout. After power changes and after a trip, the poison moves on those timescales (there is still no spatial peaking — see limits below).

Reactivity parameters are fitted to measurements, not chosen. Rod bank worths come from a Westinghouse training-simulator dataset (4068 pcm control banks, 3676 pcm shutdown banks). Moderator coefficients are least-squares fits to the three measured hot-zero-power isothermal temperature coefficients in MIT’s BEAVRS benchmark (−1.75 pcm/°F at 975 ppm boron, −4.65 at 902, −8.01 at 810), with residuals under 0.1 pcm/°F at those points. Core excess reactivity is then solved so hot-zero-power, all-rods-out critical boron lands on BEAVRS’s measured 975 ppm.

Thermal-hydraulics

  • Lumped two-node core thermal model — a fuel node and a coolant node with first-order heat transfer between them and onward to the steam generator. Decay heat is a separate source that persists after shutdown: a scrammed core is still a heat source, and the plant has to deal with it.
  • The pressurizer couples RCS inventory, thermal expansion, and steam-space thermodynamics, with heaters, spray, and relief. Geometry is taken from a real plant final safety analysis report — steam space 5.8% of RCS volume (Beaver Valley Unit 2 UFSAR) — so a given insurge or outsurge moves indicated level the way that volume says it should.
  • Degraded regimes are modeled, not fenced off. Subcooling is tracked; steam generator heat transfer collapses as the tube bundle uncovers; the primary can void; the pressurizer can go solid. The accidents worth training live in those regimes, so the model has to as well.

Secondary plant and electrical

  • Primary heat boils secondary water. Steam flow, feedwater (main and auxiliary), and steam generator level form one continuous mass-energy balance; dumps and isolation change where the heat goes.
  • Turbine, condenser, and generator close the loop. Electrical load is a steam demand: change load and the primary feels it through steam generator pressure and temperature. Condenser performance depends on circulating-water temperature. Trip the turbine or lose the heat sink and the stored energy has to go somewhere — the plant makes you deal with that.

Instrumentation and protection

Board readings are instrument channels: lag, noise, and optional stuck or drifting failures. Trips, interlocks, engineered safeguards, and automatic controllers act on those channels — not on a private true-state feed.

How we know it behaves

  • Sourced, not recalled. Plant numbers — setpoints, worths, volumes, coefficients — change with a citation to a primary document (utility FSARs, NRC standard technical specifications, measured benchmarks such as BEAVRS). Citations are recorded in the repository with the change.
  • Gated by automated tests on every change: physics acceptance scenarios, a plant behavior catalog, procedure replays through the full control stack, and checks that trip setpoints sit inside the range their instruments can indicate. The live suite lives in the repo and grows with the plant.
  • Open source. The model is AGPL-licensed and on GitHub. If you want the exact equation, read the code.

What is deliberately left out

  • No spatial core. Point kinetics: no 3-D flux map, no nodal power distribution. Power-shape management is not a task on this board.
  • One loop, lumped nodes. No subchannel thermal-hydraulics; thermal limits are lumped indicators, not hot-channel calculations.
  • No full containment engineering suite. Core-damage paths are modeled far enough to teach progression — not to analyze a licensing case.
  • Not a digital twin of any named unit. A composite plant anchored to published data from several real ones; no single unit will match it line for line.

Detail is spent where the operator feels it; the rest is simplified on purpose. Where a simplification understates reality, the in-sim manual says so.
