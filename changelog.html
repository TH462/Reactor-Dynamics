Changelog

What has changed in the simulator, newest first.

This log begins with the public launch. The years of development before that aren’t listed — there is no point cataloguing changes to something nobody could yet use.

Take it offline, and a reactor that behaves like the measurements say

Alpha 1.10.0 30 July 2026
  • AddedThe whole simulator now ships as a single HTML file you can download and run offline. No install, no server, no folder — open it from your desktop, or email it to someone. There is a Download page in the menu; the file is zipped, and the copy offered is always built from the same code as this site.
  • AddedA short guided tour on first pause, pointing at each control in place rather than burying it in a dialog, plus an Instructor that talks you through free play.
  • AddedAn About page and a rebuilt public site, and feedback now goes straight to an email address instead of a form that only produced a file.
  • AddedAn Estimated Critical Condition table in the manual — critical boron by coolant temperature and rod position, the reference a real operator works out before diluting toward criticality.
  • ChangedThe reactor’s response to temperature is now fitted to measured plant data. Startup physics tests from a real pressurized water reactor set how strongly the coolant pushes back as it heats, and how boron weakens that effect. The core is noticeably more self‑regulating: a small rod withdrawal at power now shows up as temperature rather than power, which is what a real plant does.
  • ChangedControl and shutdown rod worths are the real measured values, replacing figures roughly 2.4× larger than anything a real plant publishes.
  • FixedDiluting boron while the plant was still cold could take the reactor critical unexpectedly. Critical boron fell far too steeply as the plant heated, so a concentration that is safe hot was critical cold — and a heatup toward full power could trip on source‑range high flux. Critical boron is now nearly flat across a heatup, so boron is held steady while heating and adjusted hot.
  • FixedThe plant can heat itself from cold on pump heat alone, reactor never started — which is what a real heatup is. It previously stalled at 219 °F because pump heat was cancelled out of the steam balance.
  • FixedThe low reactor coolant flow trip reads a real flow instrument at the real 90 % setpoint, so a stuck flow indication can now mask a genuine loss of flow — as it would in a plant.
  • FixedThe manual quoted charging and letdown flows the board never showed, and its revision history had stopped being written. Both corrected, and both are now checked automatically.

The board explains itself, and the manual matches the plant

Alpha 1.9.0 29 July 2026
  • AddedPoint at anything on the board and it tells you what it is. The System Scanner names the item and describes it in one line; click the block to expand it and the same hover gives the full account — how it behaves, what it is wired to, and the mistake it invites. Covers every card, control, component, indication and alarm tile.
  • AddedEvery expanded description links into the manual at the exact section documenting that item.
  • AddedThe manual now reads in US customary units first, with SI in parentheses — 2235 psi (15.41 MPa), 579 °F (304 °C).
  • AddedA new manual chapter on the simulation itself — what the physics model computes, what it simplifies, and what it does not model at all, so an indication you expect and cannot find is understood as absent rather than hidden.
  • AddedTwo annunciators that were modelled but undocumented now have response procedures: low coolant temperature (P‑12) and reactor coolant pump cavitation.
  • ChangedA cold plant no longer annunciates its own lineup as a casualty. A Cold Shutdown start used to open with five standing alarms, two of them critical — but a cold plant is cold and depressurized and its pumps are stopped. Those now read as status and say why ("expected, plant depressurized"; "Reactor Coolant Pumps Secured"), while still showing their real readings. Hot Standby is deliberately excluded, and pumps lost to a trip still read as a trip in any mode.
  • ChangedStatus annunciators arrive already acknowledged — they report a lineup, not a demand for action. If the condition stops being the planned state of the plant, the alarm escalates and un‑acknowledges itself.
  • ChangedPartial core uncovery now damages the core. Holding the water level below the top of the fuel overheats the exposed cladding on a Three Mile Island timescale; refloading promptly still saves it.
  • ChangedThe manual documents the plant you are actually operating. A full audit against the live simulator: the reactor trip on turbine trip above 50 % power was never written down, Mode 4 and Mode 5 were still described as unavailable long after the full cooldown shipped, and two board controls were missing entirely.
  • FixedTaking the generator off line no longer trips the reactor. The OFF position of the generator selector called the turbine‑trip path, so a planned disconnect above 50 % power scrammed the plant instantly — and one during a heatup latched a trip that fired the moment power later crossed 50 %.
  • FixedThe pipes on the diagram follow the plant. Flow animation now reflects what is actually moving and how fast, rather than running whenever the line existed.
  • FixedDecay heat is always present and always tracked, and turbine load‑follow draws total thermal output rather than neutron power.
  • FixedAlarms are timestamped with the simulated time they came in, so the order of a cascade can be read off the panel afterwards.
  • FixedThe trend tiles open with a full three‑minute line already drawn instead of a stub against the right‑hand edge.

Steady gauges and a smooth trend line

Alpha 1.8.2 28 July 2026
  • FixedThe vital‑parameter tiles no longer flicker at all. They were tearing down and rebuilding their band and trend elements on every repaint, ten times a second, which lets a half‑built frame reach the screen. Those elements are now reused in place.
  • FixedThe tile trend lines are properly smooth. They take a sample on every plant step, five times more often than before, and are plotted against time rather than sample number — so the line scrolls at a constant rate instead of stretching while its buffer fills.
  • FixedThe numbers beside the bottom graph no longer overlap. Their minimum spacing was a fixed percentage that came out two pixels short of the numbers' own height, so spreading them apart still left them touching. The spacing is now measured from the real text height.

Readable gauges: no flicker, no overlap, and a temperature scale that fits the mode

Alpha 1.8.1 28 July 2026
  • FixedThe vital‑parameter tiles no longer flicker, especially during a transient. Their coloured bands follow the plant live, and un‑rounded edges were rebuilding the gauge about ten times a second.
  • FixedReadings no longer overlap their own captions. A units bug collapsed each tile's caption to a one‑pixel line, letting the number paint on top of it — and silently dropped every bold weight on the board.
  • FixedThe tile trend lines are smooth. They sampled once per frame instead of once per plant step, covering barely thirty seconds of history as a coarse staircase. They now show a true three‑minute window at any speed.
  • AddedLow coolant temperature alarm (P‑12). The board warned and tripped on high Tavg but had nothing on the cold side, so an overcooling transient lit no warning. There is deliberately no low‑Tavg trip — a PWR does not scram on low coolant temperature.
  • ChangedThe average‑coolant‑temperature tile is scaled to the mode you are in — the hot operating window when hot, a cold‑shutdown window below Mode 3 — so the green programme band is a readable width instead of a hairline.

A glowing core, a bigger board, and instruments that sit still

Alpha 1.8.0 28 July 2026
  • AddedThe core glows. The reactor vessel now renders Cherenkov radiation, driven by fission rate rather than rod position — completely dark on a shutdown reactor, growing and widening as you bring power up.
  • AddedResizable panels. Drag the inner edge of the simulator panel, or the top edge of the trend and alarm strip, to trade space with the diagram. Your sizing is remembered.
  • ChangedThe diagram is much bigger by default. The panels either side of it were both set to absorb whatever space it did not need, which shrank it from both directions at once. They now start at a fixed size and the diagram keeps the rest.
  • ChangedThe board sits still unless something is happening. Every indication is damped the way a real panel meter is, so readings drift rather than snapping between samples: at steady power the average‑coolant‑temperature tile now changes about 3 times a minute instead of 218. Reactor power stays the liveliest indication, because excore power genuinely wanders. A meter never hides a real event — a large step bypasses the damping, so a scram still reads instantly.
  • ChangedAn indication that is off now reads zero. Instrument noise scales with signal, so a stopped ECCS pump indicates a still 0 gpm instead of hunting around 1, and a shut‑down reactor's power meter sits on zero.
  • ChangedThe vital‑parameter bands follow the plant. The green band on average coolant temperature is now the sliding Tavg program — the same reference the rod controller drives to — so you can see the band you are actually holding, and it slides with load. Primary pressure follows your live pressurizer setpoint.
  • ChangedShutdown rod buttons latch. One click drives the bank fully in or out, the button holds a yellow in‑motion light while it travels, and a second click stops it where it is.
  • FixedFlow dashes in the tees and cross now flow. They were jittering back and forth instead of moving along the pipe, and no longer step at a joint.
  • FixedStraighter pipework. The pressurizer sits on its own pipe centreline, the PORV discharge drops straight into its box, and the turbine‑to‑condenser run is square.

Plant picker, RHR naming, and a steam dump that scales with the event

Alpha 1.7.1 27 July 2026
  • ChangedSteam dump now scales with the size of a load rejection. Its temperature reference slides with turbine load instead of sitting at the no‑load value, so the dump opens in proportion to what was actually rejected. A large rejection now settles at power instead of overshooting past 100 %.
  • ChangedThe residual heat removal system is called RHR everywhere. It was labelled DHR on the control and RHR on its tab.
  • FixedThe plant picker offered reactors that have no control room. Selecting BWR or RBMK inside the simulator loaded a board that was never built for them. They are now shown as coming soon.
  • FixedA stuck‑open pressurizer spray valve repaired itself. Pressing SPRAY AUTO or moving the spray slider cleared the fault. A stuck valve now stays stuck, whatever you command.
  • FixedFifteen instruments showed their internal name in the reference manual — “startup_rate” instead of “Startup Rate (SUR)”, and so on.
  • FixedThe front page said the plant was a four‑loop design. It is a single‑loop 100 MWe unit, and now says so. The page also states that the control room needs a desktop or laptop.

Steam flow on the board, trip on turbine trip

Alpha 1.7.0 27 July 2026
  • AddedSTEAM FLOW readout. Sits directly above SG FEED RATE on the same scale, so holding feedwater in MANUAL is a matter of matching two numbers instead of chasing level. It reads total main‑steam‑line flow, so it stays live with the turbine tripped and the steam dump carrying the plant.
  • AddedReactor trip on turbine trip. Above about 50 % power a turbine trip now trips the reactor. Below that it is bypassed automatically, and the plant rides the trip out on the steam dump.
  • AddedLOAD IMBAL annunciator. Lights when reactor power and turbine load diverge. Reducing power on rods alone used to overcool the plant from 304 °C to 130 °C with nothing on the board saying so.
  • AddedRelease version by the logo in the control room.
  • ChangedStartup checklist rebuilt around the 1/M approach. Six plotted points instead of three, so the prediction converges on criticality instead of running some eighty rod steps long; new steps put feedwater in automatic and block the startup trips above P‑10. The approach steps now grade on measured source‑range counts rather than reactivity.
  • ChangedRod insertion limit is power‑dependent. Not applicable below 5 % power and ramping to 70 % withdrawn at full power, instead of a flat floor that lit ROD INS LIMIT during every startup.
  • ChangedSteam‑line breaks now have a location. The standard break is downstream of the isolation valve and closing the MSIV ends it; a new break inside containment cannot be isolated at all.
  • ChangedLarger PWR board with elastic right‑hand columns, removing the dead space at wide window sizes.
  • FixedIndication noise is set per readout. One global setting had feed and steam flow dancing at ten times their display resolution while power and output were unnaturally still. Display smoothing has been removed, so a needle now lags only as far as its instrument does.
  • FixedAutomatic feedwater control could not see the steam dump. With the turbine offline or tripped it read almost no steam flow and commanded no feed while the generator boiled down.
  • FixedAuxiliary feedwater holds level in the green band. It used to settle at 21 %, inside the amber.
  • FixedFour readouts were showing fiction. Accumulator pressure was a hard‑coded number and now falls as the tank drains, SG FEED RATE showed pump demand rather than measured flow, the condensate polisher always read NORMAL, and net reactivity printed “+−0 pcm”.
  • FixedStartup no longer overshoots to about 20 % power. The startup rate alarm and the rod withdrawal block are now set where they catch a fast ascent.
  • FixedRod speed on the first step. A bank left part‑way through a step took its next step almost immediately, whatever speed was selected.
  • FixedStrip charts keep each trace in its own lane, hold still when the plant does, and no longer turn white.
  • FixedHovering a checklist step no longer pulls panels in front of the reactor vessel.

Fine rod control, timed pressurizing, startup checklists

Alpha 1.2.0 23 July 2026
  • ChangedFine-step control bank. 912 steps of travel; about 1.5 cents of reactivity per tap near critical. Old saves migrate.
  • ChangedPressurizing takes real time. A 350‑to‑600 psi setpoint step climbs over about 90 seconds.
  • AddedMode 5 → Mode 3 heatup checklist. Pumps, pressurization, instrument handoff, approach to criticality, dilution to Hot Standby.
  • ChangedStartup checklist runs to Mode 1. Carries past criticality across the 5 % boundary and puts the generator on line.
  • Fixed“Cold to Power” completion. The arrival check now declares Mode 1 reliably.

Hands-on TMI-2, calmer board

Alpha 1.0.0 23 July 2026
  • ChangedTMI-2 Part 1 is hands-on. Secure injection and isolate the stuck relief valve yourself; the stuck valve, hot tailpipe, and maintenance tag are on the board.
  • ChangedCalmer gauges. Indication noise halved; boration/dilution moves power at the boron mixing rate. Gentler rods near startup.
  • AddedKeyboard rods and finer controls. Arrow keys drive the rods; controls highlight on hover; generator-load and boron arrows step by 1 MW / 1 ppm.
  • FixedNo strobing or drifting rods. Numbers and strip chart no longer strobe after a few minutes; rods stop when you release the key.
