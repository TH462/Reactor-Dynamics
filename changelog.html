Changelog

What has changed in the simulator, newest first.

This log begins with the public launch. The years of development before that aren’t listed — there is no point cataloguing changes to something nobody could yet use.

Roomier control room & a clearer trend chart

Alpha 1.0.5 24 July 2026
  • ChangedA bigger plant diagram. The control room is now three columns — the plant diagram and vital indications (kept as large as possible), the trend chart and alarms stacked in a middle column, and the simulator panel on the right — giving the diagram far more room.
  • AddedHide the side panel. The ⛶ button hides the simulator panel and moves the time controls atop the chart/alarms column, making the diagram larger still — handy for demonstrations. Click again to bring the panel back.
  • ChangedAuto-scaling trend chart. Each line on the strip chart now zooms to its own range, so small changes are easy to see instead of sitting flat against a full-scale axis; faint gridlines were added behind the traces.
  • ChangedGenerator load starts in Manual at power. A free-play startup in Mode 1 now begins with the generator in Manual rather than load-follow, so you drive generator load yourself.

Open source, a Legal page, and a way to reach me

Alpha 1.0.4 24 July 2026
  • AddedReactor Dynamics is now open source. The simulator’s code is released under the GNU AGPL‑3.0 license, and the operator manuals and training text under Creative Commons (CC BY 4.0) — free to study, share, and build on.
  • AddedA Legal page. Terms of use, the safety disclaimer, and the licensing details now live on a dedicated page, linked from every footer.
  • AddedGet in touch. Comments, questions, or feedback? You can now reach me directly at reactordynamics@gmail.com — there’s a contact line at the bottom of the home page.

Fine rod control, honest pressurizing, and full startup checklists

Alpha 1.0.3 23 July 2026
  • ChangedReal fine control at criticality. The control bank is now a fine‑step drive (912 steps of travel): one tap near the critical point adds about a cent and a half of reactivity — like a real plant — instead of lurching power several percent. Speeds are unchanged in feel; the 1/M plot and step readouts follow the new scale, and old saves carry over.
  • ChangedPressurizing takes real time now. Raising the pressurizer pressure setpoint no longer snaps the plant there in seconds — the heaters run flat out while pressure climbs at a deliberate heatup pace (a 350 → 600 psi step takes about a minute and a half). Holding pressure through transients is as strong as ever.
  • AddedMode 5 → Mode 3 heatup checklist. A live checklist walks the whole cold‑board heatup: pumps, pressurization, instrument handoff, a gentle approach to criticality, and a dilution‑driven ride up to Hot Standby.
  • ChangedThe startup checklist now finishes the job. It carries you past criticality, across the 5 % boundary into Mode 1, At Power, and puts the generator on line.
  • Fixed“Cold to Power” can’t stall at the finish line. The mission’s arrival check sat a hair above the temperature the plant actually settles at; it now declares Mode 1 reliably.

Xenon on the chart, and clearer control readouts

Alpha 1.0.2 23 July 2026
  • AddedXenon on the strip chart. Xenon (as % of equilibrium) is now one of the trends you can plot — handy for watching the post‑trip xenon peak build and decay.
  • ChangedThe six vital gauges now match the board. The main indications up top use the same green / amber / red readout colors as the plant diagram below them.
  • ChangedAuto‑controlled inputs read grey. When a control is running on automatic, its number box turns grey to show you can’t type into it; cyan means it’s yours to set.

Hands-on TMI-2, a calmer board, and a smoother ride

Alpha 1.0.1 23 July 2026
  • ChangedTMI-2, Part 1 is now hands-on. You work the controls the way the night-shift crew did — securing injection, isolating the stuck relief valve — instead of only watching it play out. The stuck valve, its hot tailpipe, and the maintenance tag are all visible on the board now.
  • ChangedCalmer, more honest gauges. Indication noise is cut in half, and boration/dilution now moves power at the pace of the actual (slowly‑mixing) boron rather than jumping ahead of the reading. Control rods are a touch gentler near startup.
  • AddedKeyboard rods and clearer controls. Drive the control rods with the ↑/↓ keys, and every clickable control now glows when you hover it. The generator‑load and boron arrows step by 1 (MW / ppm) for fine trimming.
  • FixedNo more flicker or stuck rods. The changing numbers and strip chart no longer strobe after a few minutes of play, and holding an arrow key no longer leaves the rods drifting after you let go.

Found a bug, or want to suggest something? The feedback page comes straight to me, and there is a 💬 button inside the simulator that can attach your session so I can see what actually happened.

Reactor Dynamics is an educational simulation — not engineering software, and no substitute for real operator training.
